# Seonbae Hall of Fame

선배 (*seonbae*) — the Korean word for the elders and predecessors who walked the path before you, carrying built-in reverence in the language itself.

The Seonbae Hall of Fame honors Korean Americans who paved the way for the community — across professions, generations, and regions. Every figure in this collection has passed away; their stories remain.

---

## About this repository

This repository hosts the proof-of-concept content for the Seonbae Hall of Fame website. It contains **25 in-memoriam profiles** of Korean Americans whose lives helped shape what the Korean American community is today.

The profiles originated as a proof-of-concept set within the [NetKAL community project](https://github.com/DaeYi/NetKAL) (Council of Korean Americans), then were carried over here to anchor the standalone Seonbae HOF website.

## Editorial principles

- **Honor the predecessor.** These are people who walked a path before us. The voice respects that.
- **Decision-focused, not achievement-focused.** Each profile centers *pivots* and *moments of choice*, not résumé highlights.
- **Identity-pluralistic.** No "this is what a KA should be." Many ways to be one.
- **Honest about complications.** Include the hard parts of a life within the body of the story itself, rather than partitioning them off as separate red flags.
- **Multi-generational voices.** From the 1903–1905 plantation wave through the post-1965 immigration generation.

## Profile format

Each profile follows the same structure:

- Name + Korean name + dates + field
- `*In memoriam.*`
- One-line frame — a single evocative sentence
- Story — 2–3 short paragraphs covering origin, key chapters, contribution
- Decision moments — 3–6 bullets, each a pivot or choice
- Why this matters for the next generation — one paragraph
- Sources — verification links

## Profiles by era

### Early era — independence movement and first wave (1880s–1945)

- [Helen Ahn (이혜련)](profiles/helen-ahn.md) — half of the first Korean married couple to immigrate to the US (1902); LA matriarch; independence movement organizer (1884–1969)
- [Pyeng Koo Yoon (윤병구)](profiles/pyeng-koo-yoon.md) — Methodist minister; with Syngman Rhee, presented the Korea petition to Theodore Roosevelt at Oyster Bay, 1905 (1880–1949)
- [Maria Hwang (황마리아)](profiles/maria-hwang.md) — founded the first Korean Women's Association in Hawaii (1913); plantation educator (1865–1937)
- [Mary Paik Lee (백광선)](profiles/mary-paik-lee.md) — author of *Quiet Odyssey*, the only full-life memoir from the 1903–1905 immigration wave (1900–1995)
- [Park Yong-man (박용만)](profiles/park-yong-man.md) — founded a Korean military academy in Nebraska in 1909; militant wing of the independence movement (1881–1928)
- [Kim Chong Lim (김종림)](profiles/kim-chong-lim.md) — Sacramento Valley "Rice King"; first Korean American millionaire; funded the Willows Korean Aviation School (1884–1973)
- [Charles Ho Kim (김호)](profiles/charles-ho-kim.md) — co-founder of Kim Brothers, Inc.; first Korean American public school namesake in the US (1884–1968)
- [Harry S. Kim (김형순)](profiles/harry-s-kim.md) — co-founder of Kim Brothers, Inc.; thirty-year independence movement patron (1885–1977)
- [Ilhan New (유일한)](profiles/ilhan-new.md) — founded La Choy Food Products and Yuhan Corporation; OSS officer trained to parachute into occupied Korea (1895–1971)
- [Ha Soo Whang (황하수)](profiles/ha-soo-whang.md) — first Korean social worker in Hawaii; kept Korean dance alive through institutional advocacy (1892–1984)
- [Nodie Sohn (노디 김)](profiles/nodie-sohn.md) — first Korean woman principal in Hawaii; Korean Christian Institute (1898–1972)
- [Chung Song Lee Ahn (안정송)](profiles/chung-song-lee-ahn.md) — Honolulu women's organizer; led every major Korean women's institution in the territory (1895–1989)
- [Philip Ahn (안필립)](profiles/philip-ahn.md) — first Korean American actor in Hollywood; 180+ credits; first Asian American actor on the Hollywood Walk of Fame (1905–1978)
- [Fred Ohr (오정수)](profiles/fred-ohr.md) — only American flying ace of Korean descent in WWII; later a Chicago dentist (1919–2015)

### Mid-century — postwar, civil rights, professional pioneers (1945–2025)

- [Susan Ahn Cuddy (안수산)](profiles/susan-ahn-cuddy.md) — first Asian American woman in the US Navy; codebreaker; daughter of Dosan Ahn Chang-ho (1915–2015)
- [Young Oak Kim (김영옥)](profiles/young-oak-kim.md) — first minority officer in US history to command a combat battalion; later LA nonprofit builder (1919–2005)
- [Herbert Choy (최영조)](profiles/herbert-choy.md) — first Asian American federal judge; first person of Korean ancestry admitted to the US bar (1916–2004)
- [K. W. Lee (이경원)](profiles/kw-lee.md) — "godfather of Asian American journalism"; investigative reporter on the Chol Soo Lee case (1928–2025)
- [K. Connie Kang (강경실)](profiles/k-connie-kang.md) — first female Korean American journalist at mainstream US dailies; hired by the LA Times after Sa-I-Gu (1942–2019)
- [David Hyun (현 데이비드)](profiles/david-hyun.md) — first registered Korean American architect; designed Little Tokyo's Japanese Village Plaza (1917–2012)
- [Hi Duk Lee (이희덕)](profiles/hi-duk-lee.md) — founder of LA's Koreatown; opened Olympic Market in 1971 (1939–2019)
- [Dora Yum Kim (염도라)](profiles/dora-yum-kim.md) — placed 3,000+ Korean immigrants in their first US jobs; co-founded the first Korean community center in the US (1921–2003)
- [Luke Ik Chang Kim (김익창)](profiles/luke-ik-chang-kim.md) — pioneer of cultural psychiatry; chief psychiatrist at California's Vacaville prison hospital (1930–2015)
- [Dae-Sook Suh (서대숙)](profiles/dae-sook-suh.md) — founding director of the first US Korean Studies center (UH Manoa, 1972); wrote the definitive English-language biography of Kim Il Sung (1931–2022)
- [Yong Soon Min (민영순)](profiles/yong-soon-min.md) — pioneer Asian American visual artist; co-founded Godzilla and the Asian American Arts Alliance (1953–2024)

## Scope notes

**Korean American definition:** Strict — US-born or US-naturalized. Working interpretation for historical figures: "Korean by ancestry, primarily lived/contributed in or to the US, *or* would have been a US citizen but for the racial exclusion laws of 1882–1952." This interpretation is necessary because Asians were barred from naturalization for most of the early diaspora era; figures like Park Yong-man, Helen Ahn, and Pyeng Koo Yoon could not legally become US citizens for most of their lives.

**Why "in memoriam" only:** This repository's content is the deceased-only subset of a larger collection. Honoring the dead requires a different editorial register than profiling the living, and this site is built for that register. The broader living-and-deceased collection lives in the parent NetKAL repository.

**Source quality:** Each profile carries source URLs that were verified at the time of research. As living memory recedes and primary archives become accessible, profiles should be revisited and expanded.
