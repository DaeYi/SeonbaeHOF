# Charles Ho Kim (김호 / Kim Ho)

**Born:** 1884 — Korea (some sources say 1889)
**Died:** January 5, 1968 — Los Angeles, California
**Field:** Farmer, businessman, independence movement leader

*In memoriam.*

## One-line frame

The Reedley orchard king who ran an exclusive nectarine patent into the first Korean American million-dollar business — and spent the proceeds on a free Korea.

## Story

Charles Ho Kim taught briefly at the Pai Chai and Ewha schools in Korea before fleeing into Chinese exile in 1912. He arrived in San Francisco in 1914, made his way to the Central Valley, and in 1920 partnered with Harry S. Kim (no relation) to found Kim Brothers, Inc. in Reedley. Charles ran internal operations — orchards, product development, quality control. Harry handled sales. When the local plant breeder Fred W. Anderson developed a fuzzless peach hybrid that would eventually be marketed as the nectarine, Kim Brothers secured the exclusive 16-year cultivation license. By the 1950s the Le Grand and Sun Grand nectarines they sold dominated the market. The company hit $1.5 million in sales. They built six farms across 500 acres, a $100,000 nursery, and a $400,000 packing house, employing more than 300 workers per season. Charles and Harry are generally regarded as the first Korean American millionaires.

Charles did not treat the business as the point. From 1936 onward he was a reform leader in the Korean National Association of North America, serving three terms as Central Executive Committee Chair (1937, 1945, 1949). He personally donated $20,000 — more than half the total cost — for the construction of the KNA's Los Angeles headquarters on Jefferson Street, opened in 1938. At its dedication, he said: *"This building is a home for us displaced Koreans. Here, we will foster progress for our people, we will support the Provisional Government in liberating our homeland, and we will educate our future generations."* When Pearl Harbor put Japanese Americans into camps, Charles helped organize the Tiger Brigades — Korean American units within the California National Guard — that kept Korean Americans out of internment and into uniform. He raised $840,000 in US war bonds for the Korea Wartime Savings Committee. In 1941 he was a central figure in unifying the bitterly fractured Korean independence factions into the United Korean Committee, working alongside people he politically disagreed with, including Syngman Rhee's supporters.

After liberation, he traveled to Korea in 1945 and served on a transitional legislative commission, then returned to the US in 1948 to focus on Korean American institution-building. In 1957 he founded the Korean Foundation, Inc., whose scholarships funded more than 600 students between 1958 and 1974. He co-published *50 Years of Korean American History* in 1959. In 1963, in his late seventies, he championed the founding of the Korean Community Center in Los Angeles. He died at 84. In 2006, Charles Ho Kim Elementary School in LA became the first American public school named after a Korean American.

## Decision moments

- Fled into Chinese exile in 1912, then crossed the Pacific to California in 1914 rather than returning to a Japanese-occupied Korea
- Partnered with a fellow Korean immigrant in 1920 in a fruit business neither one had trained for, in a town with almost no Korean community
- Locked up the nectarine cultivation license — bet the company's future on a single patented variety
- Treated the million-dollar fortune as movement capital: $20,000 for the KNA headquarters, $10,000+ raised personally on independence tours, $840,000 in war bonds organized in WWII
- In 1941 swallowed his factional disagreements with Syngman Rhee to help build the United Korean Committee
- In his late seventies, kept building new institutions (Korean Community Center, 1963) instead of retiring

## Why this matters for the next generation

A lot of Korean American business success stories end at the business. Charles Ho Kim's begins there. He treated wealth as the means, not the end — and he was patient enough to do this for forty years, across an independence struggle, a world war, a homeland's liberation, a homeland's partition, and the entire founding of the Korean American institutional infrastructure in California. The reasonable thing to do with a 1950s nectarine fortune was to enjoy it. He used it to build a community.

## Red flag check

Birth year cited inconsistently (1884 in some sources, 1889 in others). His political role placed him on the anti-Rhee side of the bitter mid-century independence-movement schism, which made him a contested figure in Rhee-aligned circles for years. No personal controversies surfaced.
