# Park Yong-man (박용만)

**Born:** July 2, 1881 — Cheorwon, Gangwon Province, Korea
**Died:** October 17, 1928 — Beijing, China
**Field:** Independence activist, military strategist, journalist, educator

*In memoriam.*

## One-line frame

The Korean independence leader who tried to build an army in Nebraska — because liberation, he believed, needed soldiers more than speeches.

## Story

Park Yong-man came to the United States in 1905 after serving prison time in Korea for reformist activity. He landed in Denver, then Nebraska — a corn-and-cattle state thousands of miles from the country he meant to free — and made an unlikely bet: if Korea was ever going to get out from under Japanese occupation, Koreans abroad would need to be soldiers, not just sympathizers. He enrolled at Hastings College, then transferred to the University of Nebraska to study political and military science, graduating in 1911.

While still a student, in 1909, he founded the Youth Military Academy in Hastings — drilling Korean American students in military tactics on prairie ground that had nothing to do with Korea except for the people standing on it. He went on to organize the Korean National Brigade in Hawaii and edited Korean-language newspapers calling for armed resistance. He clashed publicly with Syngman Rhee and Ahn Chang-ho, who favored diplomatic strategies; Park believed those strategies were too slow.

He was assassinated in Beijing in 1928, at age 47, by a Korean communist — the politics of independence having moved past him in a different direction. The Korean memorial at Hastings College today is one of the few US monuments to a Korean independence figure.

## Decision moments

- Left Korea for the US after imprisonment, rather than stay underground at home
- Chose Nebraska military science over the legal or commercial tracks open to Korean immigrants
- Founded a military academy as a college student, on American soil, for a country across an ocean
- Edited insurgent newspapers and organized armed Korean units in Hawaii — chose the militant path over the diplomatic one
- Stayed in active resistance organizing in China even as the independence movement fragmented around him

## Why this matters for the next generation

You can build the future of a place you cannot enter. The Korean independence movement happened in Korea — but it also happened in Hawaii, Shanghai, San Francisco, and a tiny college town in Nebraska. Park's life is the original version of a question every Korean American eventually meets: how do you serve a community across an ocean? How do you build something for a place that isn't, and may never be, fully yours?
