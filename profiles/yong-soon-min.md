# Yong Soon Min (민영순)

**Born:** April 29, 1953 — Bugok, South Korea
**Died:** March 12, 2024 — Los Angeles, California (breast cancer)
**Field:** Visual artist, curator, educator

*In memoriam.*

## One-line frame

The artist who refused to let America forget the Korean War — and built the institutions that let other Asian American artists not have to fight that fight alone.

## Story

Yong Soon Min was born in 1953 in a village south of Seoul, in the same year an armistice — not a peace treaty — paused a war that has never officially ended. She came to the US with her family in 1960, settled in Monterey, California, and worked her way through three degrees at UC Berkeley (BA, MA, MFA) before moving to New York in the 1980s. There she became one of the founding members of Godzilla: Asian American Art Network and the Asian American Arts Alliance — grassroots organizations built in the late 1980s and early 1990s because the mainstream art world had no real space for Asian American artists, and people like Min decided to build that space themselves rather than wait for it.

Her work — installation, photography, mixed media — kept returning to the unresolved Korean War, the 38th Parallel, and the diasporic Korean body. *Make Me* (1989) presented self-portraits stamped and partially erased with words like "Exotic" and "Immigrant." *deColonization* (1991) centered a traditional Korean dress overlaid with gold lettering and panels of testimony from Korean women under US occupation. *Defining Moments* (1992) tied the 1980 Gwangju Uprising into her own iconography. She held works in the collections of MoMA, LACMA, and the Seoul Museum of Art. In 1993 she moved to Los Angeles to join the UC Irvine art faculty, where she taught until her retirement in 2014 and remained professor emerita until her death.

She mentored relentlessly. The artists and curators who passed through her studio and her classrooms describe a teacher who took every visit seriously, asked their opinion, and treated their work as worth engaging with. She was a co-founder of GYOPO, a Los Angeles collective of Korean diasporic cultural producers, in the last decade of her life. She died of breast cancer in March 2024. UCI's Langson Institute and Museum is organizing a 2027 retrospective, *Both Sides Now*.

## Decision moments

- Earned three degrees at Berkeley and then chose New York and an art career — a path with no Korean American model in front of her
- Shifted from earlier minimalist work into explicitly identity-focused, politically charged art at exactly the moment the mainstream art world rewarded the opposite
- Co-founded Godzilla and the Asian American Arts Alliance — chose to build infrastructure for other artists instead of optimizing her own career
- Took the UC Irvine post in 1993 and stayed in teaching for 20+ years — invested in the next generation rather than maximizing the gallery years
- Kept the Korean War — a war Americans treat as forgotten — as her permanent subject across four decades of work
- Founded GYOPO late in life — proved you can still be a builder in your sixties

## Why this matters for the next generation

There is a version of an art career that is only about your own work. Yong Soon Min did not have that career. She made the work — major, museum-collected, internationally exhibited — and she also made the rooms in which other Asian American artists could work, the classes that taught them, and the collectives that fought for their visibility. The lesson is not that you must do both. The lesson is that someone always does both, and the artists you grow up admiring are usually standing in a room that someone like Yong Soon Min built so they could stand there.
