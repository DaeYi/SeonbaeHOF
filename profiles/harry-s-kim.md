# Harry S. Kim (김형순 / Kim Hyung-soon)

**Born:** 1885 — Korea (some sources say 1886)
**Died:** 1977 — Los Angeles, California (age 92)
**Field:** Horticulturist, businessman, Korean National Association patron

*In memoriam.*

## One-line frame

The Pai Chai-trained interpreter who became a Reedley nursery man, co-built the first Korean American million-dollar business, and quietly funded the independence movement for thirty years.

## Story

Harry S. Kim was a product of the same generation of mission-school Koreans who built the early diaspora. He attended Pai Chai School in Seoul, the country's first modern Western school, learned English well enough to pass the official interpreter's examination, and sailed for the United States as a credentialed translator. He went back briefly to Korea to marry Hahn Duk-sae, an Ewha School graduate, and returned to California in 1913 to settle in Reedley — a hot, agricultural town in the San Joaquin Valley that the early Korean Christian community had identified as workable terrain for orchard work.

In 1920 he started a nursery and packing business focused on saplings, seedlings, and stone fruit. The same year, he partnered with Charles Ho Kim — who had once tutored Harry's wife in mathematics — to found Kim Brothers, Inc. The two men split labor cleanly: Charles ran orchards and operations; Harry ran sales, customers, and the outward face of the business. When the local breeder Fred W. Anderson developed a fuzzless peach, Kim Brothers secured exclusive cultivation rights. Their Le Grand and Sun Grand nectarines dominated the market for nearly two decades. The company grew to 500 acres across six farms, employed 300+ seasonal workers, and reached $1.5 million in sales — making Harry and Charles the first Korean American millionaires.

Harry's politics ran through the Korean National Association. He had supported Syngman Rhee financially in the early years; he broke with Rhee over Rhee's marriage to Franziska Donner and never resumed personal support. After that he poured his giving into the KNA itself — a major contributor to the 1938 KNA building on Jefferson Street in Los Angeles. Between 1914 and 1945 he documented donations of $5,337 to the Korean independence movement, a substantial sum in early-20th-century dollars given over three decades of patient, year-by-year giving. After the war he and Charles put $100,000 into a Korean student scholarship fund. He died in Los Angeles in 1977, age 92. South Korea posthumously awarded him the Order of Merit for National Foundation in 2011.

## Decision moments

- Took the official interpreter's examination as a path out of Korea, rather than staying in mission-school work at home
- Returned to Korea to marry, then immediately came back to California — committed to building a life on the American side of the Pacific
- Partnered with Charles Ho Kim in 1920 on a business neither was trained for — a 30-year partnership that never broke
- Bet the company on the nectarine cultivation license
- Cut off Syngman Rhee personally over Rhee's marriage and redirected his giving to the institutional KNA — a values-based political break, not a strategic one
- Gave for thirty years at a steady pace, not in one heroic check — built up $5,000+ for the independence movement through patient regular donation

## Why this matters for the next generation

Two-person partnerships that last thirty years are extremely rare. Two-person partnerships that produce the first Korean American million-dollar business and fund a national-liberation movement at the same time are unique. Harry S. Kim is the quieter half of the Kim Brothers story — the operations and sales half, the steady-giving half, the one whose name fewer people know. That asymmetry is itself instructive. Movements get built by both kinds of people: the visible Charles Ho Kims and the steady Harry S. Kims. The community needs both. Being the second name on the partnership is not a smaller life.
