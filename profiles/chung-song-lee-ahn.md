# Chung Song Lee Ahn (안정송)

**Born:** 1895 — Pyongyang, Korea
**Died:** 1989 — Honolulu, Hawaii
**Field:** Educator, women's independence organizer, YWCA leader

*In memoriam.*

## One-line frame

Ewha graduate who landed in Honolulu in January 1919 — two months before the March First Movement broke open in Seoul — and spent the next seventy years running every Korean women's organization in the islands.

## Story

Chung Song Lee was born in Pyongyang in 1895, graduated from Ewha Hakdang in 1913, then went to Japan and completed the Japan Women's Seminary in Yokohama in 1918. She taught briefly at the Pyongyang Women's Seminary, then sailed for Honolulu in January 1919. Two months later, the March First Movement erupted in Seoul: protests, declarations of independence, mass arrests. She was already in Hawaii, and the rest of her life was organized around the question of what a Korean woman in Hawaii owed to a Korea she could not return to under occupation.

Over the next several decades she became the connective tissue of Korean women's institutional life in the territory. She chaired the Korean YWCA. She was president of the Korean Women's Relief Society — the women's wing of the independence movement that raised money and sent it across the Pacific. She presided over the women's branch of the Korean National Association (Women Kuk Min Hur), the largest Korean political body in the United States. She led the Ewha Women's University Alumnae Association of Hawaiʻi. She represented the Korean community on the United Welfare Fund Drive. The phrase "active in most Korean organizations in the state" appears in nearly every biographical sketch of her, and it is not hyperbole.

She lived to 94 and died in Honolulu in 1989. In 2021, more than three decades after her death, the South Korean government posthumously honored her as a "woman patriot," in a ceremony at the UH Center for Korean Studies, alongside Nodie Kim.

## Decision moments

- Took the Ewha-to-Japan-to-Pyongyang teaching track at a moment when educated Korean women were rare and locally consequential
- Left Korea for Honolulu in January 1919 — and was already abroad when March First happened, which redefined what her education was for
- Stayed in Hawaii instead of returning to occupied Korea — and turned diaspora organizing into a full vocation
- Joined the YWCA structure rather than building a separate Korean-only women's institution — chose interracial respectability politics as one of her platforms
- Worked across the spectrum of Korean Hawaii organizations rather than aligning with a single faction — preserved her usefulness to a community that was fractured between Rhee and Park Yong-man wings
- Continued the organizational work into her old age — Ewha alumnae leadership into the postwar decades

## Why this matters for the next generation

You can build a full Korean life thousands of miles from Korea, in a country that is not yours, in service of a country you may never see again — and that life can be its own thing, not a consolation prize. Chung Song Lee Ahn was an Ewha graduate, a YWCA chair, a Korean National Association women's president, and an alumnae leader all at once, in Honolulu, for seventy years. The lesson is not "wait until you can go home." The lesson is: home is the thing you build where you are, and the work of building it is the contribution.
